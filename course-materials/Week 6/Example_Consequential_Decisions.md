# Example Consequential Decisions for Week 6

The purpose of this exercise is to help you identify a decision that meaningfully changes your career strategy, not just a small task or a minor adjustment. A consequential decision is a choice that changes the direction, order, or assumptions of your plan in a way that has real implications for your next steps.

For each example below, notice how the student:
- identifies a decision that changes the plan,
- points to a source that made the decision necessary,
- names the uncertainty that remains,
- and explains how the decision changes their map.

---

## Example 1: Narrowing a target role

- **Consequential decision:** I changed my map from “game designer” to “technical artist.”
- **Source that informed it:** A set of job postings and professional portfolio examples showing that technical artists often combine visual design, game-engine knowledge, and problem-solving between artists and programmers.
- **Uncertainty:** I am not yet sure whether I prefer the artistic side, the technical side, or the bridge between them.
- **Short explanation:** My earlier map treated game design as one broad goal. After comparing roles, I realized that “game designer” and “technical artist” require different forms of preparation. I revised the map to focus on technical art because it better connects my visual interests with the real-time tools I am beginning to learn. I still need to test whether I enjoy the technical parts enough to commit to this direction.

---

## Example 2: Adding a skill milestone

- **Consequential decision:** I added a Unity or Unreal portfolio milestone before applying for internships.
- **Source that informed it:** An internship description that listed real-time engine experience and a portfolio project as preferred qualifications.
- **Uncertainty:** I do not know whether one small project will be enough evidence for the kinds of internships I want.
- **Short explanation:** My earlier map moved directly from coursework to internship applications. The source made me realize that I need visible proof of my skills, not just a statement that I am interested in interactive media. I added a portfolio milestone so that my strategy includes producing a small playable or interactive project before applying.

---

## Example 3: Revising the order of steps

- **Consequential decision:** I moved networking and collaborative project work earlier in my plan.
- **Source that informed it:** A professional profile or interview in which someone described entering the field through team projects, referrals, and shared portfolio work.
- **Uncertainty:** I am not sure which communities, events, or collaborators will be most accessible to me.
- **Short explanation:** My first map assumed that I would build skills first and make professional connections later. The source suggested that collaboration and visibility are part of preparation, not something that happens only after I am “ready.” I changed the order of my map so that I begin joining projects, attending events, or contacting peers earlier.

---

## Example 4: Keeping a decision but making it conditional

- **Consequential decision:** I kept graduate school on the map, but changed it from an automatic goal to a conditional option.
- **Source that informed it:** A graduate program description and several job postings showing that some roles require advanced study, while others emphasize experience, portfolio work, or technical skills.
- **Uncertainty:** I do not yet know whether the specific role I choose will require graduate study.
- **Short explanation:** My earlier map assumed graduate school was the next step after my degree. After looking at sources, I realized that graduate school may be valuable for research-oriented or specialized roles, but it may not be necessary for every career direction I am considering. I revised the map so that graduate school depends on the kind of work I decide to pursue and the evidence I still need.

---

## Example 5: Adding a contingency

- **Consequential decision:** I added a backup pathway that connects my media skills to production coordination or human-centered AI workflows.
- **Source that informed it:** An article, report, or class reading discussing automation, AI tools, and changing production workflows in media industries.
- **Uncertainty:** I do not know which tasks in my target field will change most quickly or which new roles will become stable.
- **Short explanation:** My earlier map assumed that the role I want will remain mostly the same. The source made me consider how tools may change the work even if the field does not disappear. I added a contingency pathway so that I can continue developing media skills while also learning how to work across teams, tools, and changing production systems.

---

## Example 6: Identifying a vulnerable assumption

- **Consequential decision:** I marked local sound-design work as a vulnerable part of my plan and added remote or adjacent opportunities as alternatives.
- **Source that informed it:** A location-specific job search showing fewer local sound-design postings than expected, along with more remote or adjacent media-production roles.
- **Uncertainty:** I do not know whether the limited results reflect a temporary hiring pattern or a longer-term constraint.
- **Short explanation:** My earlier map assumed that I could find local sound-design opportunities after building my portfolio. The source made that assumption less certain. I did not abandon sound design, but I revised the map to include related roles, freelance possibilities, remote work, and additional networking as ways to reduce dependence on one local market.

---

## Framing reminder for your own work

When you prepare your consequential decision for this activity, aim to include:

1. **The decision:** What changed in your map or plan?
2. **The source:** What evidence made this decision necessary?
3. **The uncertainty:** What you are still not sure about.
4. **The explanation:** Why this change improves your strategy.

A strong consequential decision is one that changes the structure of your plan, not simply adds a minor task. It should show that you are actively adapting your strategy based on evidence, not just listing what you may do next.
