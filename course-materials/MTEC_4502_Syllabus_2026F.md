## Course Description

Prepares students for the transition from academic life to the professional world. Topics covered include selection and presentation of portfolio items, resume preparation, interviewing skills, budgeting and financial concerns, and how to navigate a first job. For hybrid and online sections, minimum technology requirements are a working camera and microphone. Students are to switch both on at the instructor's request.

Through a series of scaffolded projects—including a reflective career essay, résumé, professional portfolio, cover letter, and budgeting exercise—students develop the tools, strategies, and professional materials necessary for career placement and advancement. The course emphasizes self-evaluation, readiness analysis, and the ability to communicate professional identity across diverse platforms.

Students select a portfolio platform appropriate to their goals and learn to present their work effectively in digital and professional contexts. Throughout the semester, they will engage in cycles of drafting, critique, and revision, gaining feedback from peers and the instructor. Each project is approached as an iterative development process, with emphasis on reflection, planning, production, and assessment.

Collaborative AI is integrated into the workflow, encouraging students to explore how AI can serve as a partner in brainstorming, drafting, design, and revision—while also critically evaluating its limitations and documenting its contributions. In addition, a budgeting module introduces resource management as a core professional skill, expanding the concept beyond money to include time, space, and quality trade-offs.

By the end of the semester, students will have assembled a professional portfolio, articulated a strategic career development plan, and practiced presenting their materials in professional contexts, positioning them to enter the workforce or pursue graduate study with confidence.

### Late Work Policy

The materials you create in this course—résumé, portfolio, cover letter, reflective/strategic essay, and budgeting framework—are professional development documents that will continue to evolve throughout your career. Because of this, none are ever "complete," and assessment is based on the materials as they exist at the time of the final pitch presentation.

* **Iterative Development:** Students are expected to revise and refine their materials continuously across the semester. Missing an intermediate deadline means losing valuable critique and feedback opportunities, but the work will still be reviewed in whatever form it exists at the pitch.
* **Assessment Timing:** Final grading is based on the state of materials at the final pitch. Documents not available at that time cannot be assessed.
* **Implications for Late Work:** Revisions may be made at any point up until the pitch. However, postponing work reduces feedback opportunities and can weaken the final presentation.

## Detailed AI Use Statement

### Overview

In this course, AI is recognized as a collaborative partner in analysis, reflection, design, evaluation, and iteration—not as a substitute for student authorship, judgment, or understanding. This course treats AI fluency as a form of professional literacy essential for emerging fields.

### Guiding Principles

* **Transparency:** Students must clearly indicate when and how AI tools have been used.
* **Attribution:** Any text, analysis, structure, or visual material generated or shaped by AI must be attributed, including tool name and version.
* **Agency and Responsibility:** AI may support thinking, but final decisions, interpretations, and accountability always rest with the student.
* **Understanding Requirement:** If you do not understand an AI-generated output, you may not use it.
* **Ethics and Integrity:** Students are responsible for ensuring AI use complies with academic integrity standards.

### Acceptable Uses of AI

* Brainstorming and speculative exploration of career paths.
* Comparative analysis of portfolio platforms or professional materials.
* Organizational support, including tables, outlines, checklists, and summaries.
* Feedback and critique on clarity, tone, and structure.
* Assistance with documentation formatting, including Markdown and tables.

### Prohibited Uses of AI

* Submitting AI-generated work that the student does not understand.
* Representing AI-generated content as wholly original without disclosure.
* Using AI to fabricate experiences, credentials, or outcomes.

### Required Documentation

Whenever AI tools are used, students must include an AI Use Acknowledgment describing the tools used, the purpose, and the nature of the contribution. This should appear on a portfolio page/footer, README, or appendix.

### Student Choice

Students may elect not to use AI, with no grade penalty, provided that they document that choice and complete the remaining assignment requirements.
