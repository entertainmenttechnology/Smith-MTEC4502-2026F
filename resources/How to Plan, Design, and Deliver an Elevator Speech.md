# **How to Plan, Design, and Deliver an Elevator Speech**

An elevator speech (or elevator pitch) is a concise, persuasive 30- to 60-second introduction that communicates who you are, what you do, and the value you bring. This guide covers every stage from preplanning to live delivery.

## **1\. Preplanning & Opportunity Mapping**

Before writing a single word, clarify your objective and understand your environment. Effective preplanning ensures your pitch feels relevant rather than rehearsed.

> * **Identify Your Core Goal:** Decide what you want to happen after the conversation ends (e.g., landing an informational interview, securing a sales demo, getting a referral, or exchanging contact details).  
> * **Map Potential Opportunities:** Anticipate where you might use your pitch—networking mixers, career fairs, industry conferences, casual coffee shop encounters, or virtual meetings.  
> * **Research Your Audience:** Know the common pain points, priorities, and language of the people you expect to meet so you can frame your value around their needs.  
> * **Audit Your Proof Points:** List 2 to 3 concrete wins, metrics, or credentials that quickly build credibility without sounding boastful.

## **2\. Approaching & Opening the Conversation**

How you enter a conversation matters just as much as what you say once you have someone's attention.

| Context | Recommended Approach | Example Opener |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Networking Event | Look for open body language or people standing near refreshments; lead with curiosity about them first. | "Hi, I'm \[Name\]. What brought you to tonight's event?" |
| Career Fair / Recruiter | Make confident eye contact, offer a firm handshake, and state your target interest clearly. | "Hi, I'm \[Name\]. I've been following \[Company\]'s work in \[Area\] and wanted to introduce myself." |
| Warm Introduction | Acknowledge the mutual connection immediately to establish instant trust. | "Hi \[Name\], \[Mutual Contact\] suggested we connect because we're both working on \[Topic\]." |
| Unexpected / Casual | Keep it light, brief, and respectful of their time before launching into your pitch. | "I realize you're on your way out, so I'll keep this to 30 seconds..." |

## **3\. Designing Your Elevator Speech**

Aim for roughly **75 to 90 words**—short enough to deliver comfortably in 30 to 45 seconds without rushing.

> 1. **The Hook (5–10 seconds):** State your name, current role or focus, and an engaging statement that sparks curiosity.  
> 2. **The Value & Proof (15–20 seconds):** Explain the specific problem you solve, who you help, and one measurable result or concrete example.  
> 3. **The Differentiator (5–10 seconds):** Briefly highlight what sets your approach, background, or solution apart.  
> 4. **The Call-to-Action (5–10 seconds):** End with an open-ended question or clear next step to turn a monologue into a two-way dialogue.

| Pro Tip: Replace industry buzzwords with everyday language. If a listener outside your field cannot immediately picture what you do, simplify your phrasing. |

## **4\. Delivery & Execution**

A great script falls flat without strong vocal and physical delivery.

> * **Pacing & Pauses:** Speak at a steady, conversational pace. Pause briefly after your hook and before your closing question to let key points land.  
> * **Vocal Variety:** Emphasize action verbs and key outcomes so your voice sounds engaged rather than monotone.  
> * **Body Language:** Maintain natural eye contact, keep an open posture, and smile genuinely.  
> * **Active Listening:** Watch for cues from your listener. If they look intrigued, invite them to share their perspective; if they seem pressed for time, wrap up cleanly and ask for their card or LinkedIn.

## **5\. Pre-Event Readiness Checklist**

> * ☐ Clarify my primary goal for the upcoming event or week.  
> * ☐ Customize my hook and proof point for my target audience.  
> * ☐ Time my speech out loud to confirm it stays under 45 seconds.  
> * ☐ Prepare one low-friction follow-up question to keep the dialogue going.  
> * ☐ Have my LinkedIn QR code, digital card, or resume ready to share.
