# Portfolio Evaluation Questions

Use these questions to guide a curious, constructive review of a peer's portfolio. You do not need to answer every question. Choose the ones most relevant to the portfolio and the author's feedback request, and ground your comments in something you actually saw.

## Audience and professional story

- Who do you think this portfolio is meant to reach?
- What skills, services, or professional direction do you understand from the first page?
- What is one idea or impression you are likely to remember about this person?
- Does the bio or introduction connect the student's interests, experience, and goals? What could make that connection clearer?

## Navigation, design, and accessibility

- Can you quickly tell where to go to learn about the student's work?
- Is the organization or navigation predictable and easy to follow?
- Does the visual design support the work, or does anything distract from it?
- Is text easy to read, and do images or media have enough context?
- Does the portfolio appear usable on different screen sizes or with different access needs? What would you check or improve?

## Featured projects and evidence

- Can you tell what the student's role and contribution were in the project?
- Does the project explain the process, tools, decisions, and outcome—not just show the final artifact?
- What evidence would help you better understand the project's quality or impact?
- Is it clear what the student learned or would do differently?
- Which project would you feature first for the intended audience, and why?

## Professional readiness and next steps

- Are the résumé, contact information, and relevant professional links easy to find?
- Does the chosen platform seem appropriate for the student's goals and type of work?
- What important information might a potential employer, client, or collaborator still need?
- What is one revision that would make the portfolio more effective for its intended audience?
- What is already working well that the student should preserve as they revise?

## Suggested Comment Pattern

For each classmate's post, identify one effective element, ask or respond to one useful question, and give one specific next-step suggestion. For example:

> I understood that you are aiming this portfolio at ___. The ___ project stood out because ___. I wondered ___. One change that could help your audience is ___.

Keep comments respectful and actionable. Focus feedback on the work and its intended audience, not on the student's personal qualities. The goal is to support revision, not rank classmates.
