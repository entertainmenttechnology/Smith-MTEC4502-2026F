# Assignment 1d: AI Collaboration and Career Futures

**Course:** MTEC 4502 – Career and Portfolio Seminar  
**Instructor:** Dr. David B. Smith  
**Due:** Before the next class, through the corresponding Brightspace assignment link  
**Builds on:** [Assignment 1b](01b_Strategic_Framework.md) and [Assignment 1c](01c_Integrating_Visualization.md)

## Purpose

You have described your interests, studied current job advertisements, identified gaps, and visualized your present career strategy. Now bring another intelligence into that process. Use an AI system to question your conclusions, suggest alternatives, and help you speculate about possible versions of your career. Your task is to decide what the exchange contributes and where its claims exceed the available evidence.

AI use is **required for this assignment**. You may choose the system and decide which career material to share with it. You remain responsible for your sources, interpretations, decisions, and final writing. This assignment prepares your model for the next class, when we will examine Future of Work evidence and test the speculative possibilities more systematically.

## What to bring into the conversation

Start with a compact, accurate summary of your existing work. Include your current career direction or competing directions, the patterns you found in your 3–5 job advertisements, the most consequential gaps in your skills chart, and your interpretation of your SWOT and radar. Include personal constraints or priorities that materially affect the strategy. Give the AI enough context to analyze *your* model, while keeping a record of which statements come from sources, which are your interpretations, and which remain uncertain.

Link or cite the original advertisements and other external sources through Zotero, as required in Parts 1b and 1c. An AI response is a record of the interaction, not a source of verified job-market facts. Remove personal details about other people and confidential material before sharing your work with an AI service.

## Part 1: Interrogate the current strategy

Conduct an AI exchange that examines the reasoning behind your current strategy. Ask the system to distinguish evidence from inference; identify assumptions, contradictions, or overlooked opportunities; and propose questions that would help you decide what to do next. Do not ask it simply to produce a better career plan. Follow up on at least one challenge so that the exchange responds to your judgment rather than ending with the first answer.

You may adapt this starting prompt:

> Here is my career direction and the evidence I used to develop it: [your concise summary]. Separate what my sources directly support from my interpretations and assumptions. Identify up to three conclusions that need closer examination, including an overlooked option or contradiction if you find one. Explain which information led you to each observation and what additional evidence would test it. Ask for missing information. Do not invent job-market facts or rewrite my plan.

Select **one consequential AI observation** and check it against an original source, your own experience or artifact, or new evidence saved to Zotero. State whether you accept it, reject it, or leave it open, and why.

## Part 2: Speculate about possible career futures

Use AI to explore **at least two distinct, plausible career trajectories** over the next three to five years. One may extend your current target role; another should change a significant condition, such as the mix of creative and technical work, the organizations you serve, the tools you use, or how you enter the field. These are scenarios for thinking, not predictions or AI-generated job listings.

For each trajectory, ask the AI to explain:

1. Which of your current strengths and interests remain valuable.
2. Which gaps, experiences, portfolio evidence, or relationships would matter most.
3. What would have to happen for the trajectory to become plausible.
4. Which parts rest on your present research and which are speculation.

Challenge at least one proposal: tell the AI what seems implausible, undesirable, or inconsistent with your values or constraints, and ask it to revise its reasoning. You may use this prompt to begin:

> Based on my current strategy and evidence, propose two meaningfully different career trajectories for the next three to five years. Treat them as conditional possibilities. For each, identify the assumptions, the evidence I already have, the evidence I would need, and a small step I could take now that would be useful across more than one possible future. Do not present invented roles, salaries, or forecasts as established facts.

You do **not** need to conduct the full Future of Work research or settle which future is most likely this week. Bring one promising scenario and one unresolved question to the next class for testing against external evidence.

## Part 3: Evaluate the collaboration and make a decision

Write a **400–600 word analysis** that uses specific moments from your exchanges to explain what happened to your thinking. Address the following in connected prose:

- What did AI notice or propose that you had not considered? Where did it repeat, distort, or overstate your evidence?
- How did your questions, corrections, or constraints change its response? Identify a moment of substantive exchange, as distinct from an operation such as formatting or summarizing.
- Which AI claim did you verify, using what source or experience, and with what result?
- What will you revise in your strategic framework, keep with stronger justification, or investigate next? Explain the decision.
- Which speculative trajectory is worth further examination, and what remains uncertain about it?

You may revise your framework or visual if the analysis warrants it. A reasoned decision **not** to change an element is also acceptable. Keep your original 1a reflection intact as the unassisted baseline; document changes in a new version rather than overwriting that record.

## Submission

Submit one document or PDF through Brightspace containing:

1. A brief summary of the career evidence you supplied to AI, with links or citations to the relevant Zotero items and your 1c visuals.
2. A selective **AI interaction record**: system and date, the initial prompts, at least one follow-up or correction in each part, and excerpts that support your analysis. A full transcript may be linked, but it does not replace your interpretation.
3. Your 400–600 word analysis and decision.
4. Two short scenario summaries, each clearly marking **supported observations**, **assumptions**, and **questions for future research**.
5. A revised strategic-framework or visual link if you made a change; otherwise state why the existing version stands for now.

Keep the working files and links in your personal course workspace. Cite every external source used to substantiate a claim through Zotero. Label AI-generated speculation as such; do not cite a speculative suggestion as evidence that a job or trend exists.

## How this work will be assessed

Assessment will consider the quality of your evidence and source tracing; how specifically you questioned and corrected the AI; whether your scenarios differ meaningfully and disclose their assumptions; the care you took in checking a consequential claim; and the reasoning behind your revision or decision to retain your strategy. The number of prompts, enthusiasm for AI, and agreement with its suggestions are not measures of quality.
